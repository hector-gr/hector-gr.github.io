Abstract

Multimodal models can look at images, but they usually reason only with text. This limits what they can solve when the answer depends on changing or combining visual information. A model may need to combine two room views, reveal a hidden region, or show where an object will move.

We introduce ReImaGin. It lets a multimodal model ask an image generator for a helpful new image, then reason over that image. Across six visual tasks, ReImaGin outperforms text-only reasoning and specialist vision tools, with gains of up to 25 points.

Method

ReImaGin uses image generation as visual reasoning: the model calls a flexible, instruction-following image generator to create the visual intermediate a problem requires.

Qualitative Examples

These examples reproduce the questions, image instructions, generated views, and answers shown in the paper. The paper reports the direct baseline answers but not textual baseline traces, so none are added here.

Collision prediction

User

How big is the thing that the small car will collide if it moving forward?

ReImaGin · Reasoning summary

I need to visualize the object’s trajectory before identifying what it will hit.

generate_image("Draw a red arrow starting from the back of the cyan cruiser (motorcycle) and extending straight backward along its current heading to show its path.", image_1)

Answer: Purple ✓

Depth comparison

User

Which point is closer to the camera: A or B?

ReImaGin · Reasoning summary

I need a depth map to compare the two marked points.

generate_image("high-contrast grayscale depth map", image_1)

Answer: A ✓

ReImaGin discovers the best visual reasoning strategy.

Different problems call for different generated images: a panorama for spatial reasoning, or colored lines for path tracing. Instead of specifying these strategies by hand, ReImaGin can propose natural-language image instructions, test them on a small development set, and refine the most useful ones.

Beats text-only reasoning and specialized tools across visual reasoning tasks.
